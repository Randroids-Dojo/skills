# YouTube Title + Thumbnail Packaging

Develop title-and-thumbnail packages as inseparable pairs. Especially suited to creator, game-development, software, experiment, and documentary-style videos.

## Core principle: the double barrel

Use Derek Muller/Veritasium's method (explained at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tpQZLCel_kg&t=1133s). The title and thumbnail are two complementary shots:

- The title usually supplies the subject, premise, stakes, surprising claim, or story turn.
- The thumbnail supplies a *different* concrete detail, contradiction, emotional reaction, visual mystery, or decision.
- Together they create tension and become more compelling than either alone.
- Never merely shorten or repeat the title in the thumbnail. If both elements communicate the same fact, one barrel is wasted.
- The pairing must make the actual subject understandable — not create empty mystery.

Examples of the pattern:

| Title | Thumbnail | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The Man Who Accidentally Killed the Most People in History" | "WHOOPS!" | Title supplies the enormous consequence; thumbnail adds ironic human error. |
| "We've Been Using the Wrong Science in Court for 50 Years" | "HOW IS THIS ALLOWED?" | Title supplies the factual problem; thumbnail supplies the emotional objection. |
| "Google Maps Is Unreasonably Fast. Let Me Explain" | "HOW?" | Title makes the surprising claim; thumbnail turns it into an immediate question. |
| "AI Built My Zombie Game in an Hour. Then I Read the Code." | "5,143 LINES?!" | Title supplies the creation-to-consequence story; thumbnail supplies the shocking scale. |

## Required workflow

### 1. Ground the actual video

Inspect the supplied brief, transcript, rough cut, footage notes, source project, or user description. Identify:

- The video's real subject.
- The audience-facing promise.
- The strongest anomaly, contradiction, transformation, or decision.
- The most visually legible moment.
- Which facts and outcomes are verified, and which remain unknown.
- What the first 30 seconds actually pay off.

**Do not invent results, numbers, stakes, emotions, failures, or success.**

### 2. Identify the two barrels

Write a one-sentence definition for each:

- **Title barrel:** what information or tension the title contributes.
- **Thumbnail barrel:** what *new* information or tension the image and optional text contribute.

Reject the concept if the barrels are redundant.

### 3. Generate complete pairs

Produce title-and-thumbnail concepts as **locked pairs**, not separate title and thumbnail lists. Do not later mix a title from one concept with a thumbnail from another merely because each scored well independently.

### 4. Rank the strongest concepts

Recommend one primary pair and normally two or three meaningful alternatives. Each alternative should represent a distinct strategic angle, such as: transformation or consequence; verdict or decision; mystery or contradiction; unexpected scale; human reaction; before-and-after improvement; outcome-dependent packaging after the final edit.

### 5. Explain why each pair works

For every proposed pair, fill every field of the output format below — the fields are the explanation.

## Title guidance

- Prefer a clear, natural spoken sentence or question over a stack of keywords.
- Make the subject understandable without relying on channel familiarity.
- Lead with the surprising claim, action, consequence, or unresolved decision.
- Use concrete nouns and verified numbers when they materially improve the hook.
- Avoid vague phrases such as "You Won't Believe," "The Truth About," or "This Changes Everything" unless the video genuinely earns them.
- Avoid beginning with channel branding, episode numbers, or insider terminology.
- Keep titles readable in recommendation feeds; remove words that do not add subject, stakes, or tension.
- Do not make an uncertain outcome sound settled.
- Do not spoil a verdict unless the finished cut supports an outcome-forward package and the spoiler makes the story more compelling.
- A title may create curiosity, but it must accurately describe the video viewers receive.

## Thumbnail guidance

- Use one dominant visual subject.
- Use one face at most, and only when the expression remains legible at phone size.
- Prefer real footage, real objects, or truthful visual reconstruction.
- Avoid generic AI robots, glowing brains, code walls, logos, and decorative clutter unless one is genuinely central to the story.
- Use strong silhouette, separation, contrast, scale, and negative space.
- Arrows or circles should clarify a real visual relationship, not manufacture importance.
- Thumbnail text should usually be one to four instantly readable words or one compact number.
- No text is acceptable when the image already supplies a strong second barrel.
- The thumbnail text must add something the title does not already say.
- Avoid placing multiple statistics, captions, logos, labels, or competing focal points in one image.
- Assume the viewer sees the thumbnail briefly and at phone size.

## Outcome-aware packaging

When the result is not yet known, separate packaging into:

- **Pre-production pairs** that truthfully frame the experiment or decision.
- **Post-edit alternatives** that may be used only if the completed video earns the claim.

Never let a provisional title predetermine the experiment's conclusion. Mark evidence-dependent options clearly.

## Validation test

Check every candidate pair:

1. **Title alone:** Does it identify the subject and establish a story or question?
2. **Thumbnail alone:** Can its visual idea be understood in about one second?
3. **Non-redundancy:** Does the thumbnail add a genuinely different second shot?
4. **Combined tension:** Does seeing both change, intensify, or complicate the viewer's understanding?
5. **Mobile readability:** Are the focal subject and any words readable at small size?
6. **Truthfulness:** Are every number, visual implication, and outcome supported?
7. **Opening payoff:** Do the first 30 seconds acknowledge and begin paying off both barrels?
8. **Full-video payoff:** Does the video reach the promised answer, transformation, or verdict?
9. **Audience clarity:** Can someone unfamiliar with the channel understand why the video matters?
10. **Distinctness:** Is this a coherent concept rather than a generic YouTube template?

Reject any pair that fails truthfulness, subject clarity, or opening payoff.

## When editing a production brief

- Preserve existing unrelated sections, styles, links, and structure.
- Replace separate title and thumbnail brainstorms with complete paired concepts.
- Add the double-barrel rule and validation test compactly.
- Keep research sources as readable hyperlinks.
- Verify the final document structure and, when layout-sensitive, inspect an exported PDF for clipping, broken tables, orphaned headings, and awkward page breaks.
- Do not upload, publish, schedule, change a live YouTube video, or modify channel metadata without explicit authorization.

## Tone

Be direct, creative, skeptical, and specific. Optimize for clarity and earned curiosity rather than maximum sensationalism. Treat the title and thumbnail as the beginning of the story, not advertising detached from it.

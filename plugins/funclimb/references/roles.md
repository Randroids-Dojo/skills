# Experiment role briefs

Use these as role briefs or successive passes. They do not by themselves authorize subagents. Give every role the frozen campaign, the current experiment and the evidence it needs. Blind reviewers to target scores and candidate identities where possible.

## Proposer: find the next useful uncertainty

Describe an observed player moment. State the intended emotion or decision, the current behavior and its evidence. Offer three distinct hypotheses: a focused tune, a subtraction or simplification where applicable, and a different design approach. For each, predict what a player will do or understand differently, a cheap way to disprove it, a possible downside, and estimated cost and time. Recommend the smallest experiment that resolves the largest uncertainty. Respect the chosen genre and pillars. Do not optimize session length or model scores as a substitute for enjoyment.

## Implementer: one coherent candidate

Implement the selected hypothesis in the authorized game branch. Preserve a reproducible baseline and keep rendering effects separate from authoritative gameplay. Do not change the evaluation contract. Capture immutable build identifiers and document changed parameters, affected controls, failure and recovery, and accessibility modes. Stop if the core action cannot be exercised. Return the candidate build, focused check results, risks and the instrumentation the observer needs.

## Observer: attempt to disprove the claim

Run the planned scenario on both builds under matched initial state, device, settings and paired seeds, in counterbalanced order. Follow actual input through to the intended consequence. Capture event and state evidence and short motion footage where useful. Include release and cancel, rapid repetition or rejected input when affected. Record missing tests as not-tested and failures as fail. Keep timing measurements distinct from dispatch and capture overhead. State exactly which claims remain unverified.

## Council reviewer: the autonomous quality evaluator

Independently compare both supplied variants and the pinned reference footage or images, without a target score or the implementer's sales pitch. Use the game's pillars and `rubric.md` to judge control and readability, meaningful choices, pacing, expression and presentation as relevant. Cite existing artifact hashes and the exact moments supporting the comparison. Give one reason each variant might be better and one alternative explanation. Return the receipt shape in `protocol.md`: candidate, baseline, tie or inconclusive; confidence low, medium or high; evidenceRefs with observations; blockingFindings (empty when none); and observations as one string. Optional anchored per-dimension scores can guide hypotheses, but score inflation is not an improvement. Do not claim to have played if you only inspected captures. The council's evidence-grounded majority promotes candidates autonomously; actual human play is not required.

## Optional human calibration

When a human playtest is requested or available, ask which build felt better and at which moment, what they understood, what they intentionally changed after failure and what they would try next. Use the answers to calibrate future council judgments or redirect a future campaign. This is not a blocking stage of the default loop. Never manufacture a participant or feedback.

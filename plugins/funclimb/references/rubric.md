# Finding the intended fun

Define the audience, context and experience before evaluating. Fun can be mastery, tactical deliberation, discovery, expression, tension, comedy, social cooperation or a quiet ritual. Preserve intended difficulty and pacing. A card game can stay still between decisions.

## Experience dimensions, kept separate

- **Agency and control:** Did the intended action happen? Can the player predict, choose and influence its consequence? Does failure teach a different next action?
- **Legibility and feedback:** Can the player perceive what happened and why through the actual camera and interface? Are acknowledgment and delayed execution distinguished?
- **Meaningful challenge:** Does uncertainty create an interesting decision at the intended skill level? Frustration can be intentional; confusion caused by broken controls is a different finding.
- **Expression and discovery:** Are multiple approaches, styles or strategies viable where intended? Does experimentation reveal something useful or surprising?
- **Pacing and recovery:** Is the time spent anticipating, acting, resolving and retrying appropriate? Test a changed rhythm rather than assuming faster is always better.
- **Sensory quality:** Do visuals, motion, audio and haptics support the action without obscuring it? Verify each modality with suitable evidence.
- **Coherence and accessibility:** Does the change preserve the game's identity and the affected players' ability to play? Evaluate reduced motion, readability and alternate controls where relevant.

## No universal fun score

For each relevant dimension, record an observed moment, its consequence, confidence, counterevidence and a testable next hypothesis. Use pairwise preference with a rationale rather than averaging scores into a win. Bot success rate, frame rate, retention and screenshot ratings are diagnostic signals; none alone proves enjoyment. Qualitative assessment begins with the first playable slice.

## Council estimates drive the loop

The council may judge likely play quality from captures, traces, active play where available, and reference material, and promote improvements autonomously. Evaluate controls and readable consequences, meaningful decisions, pacing, expression and sensory quality as relevant to the game. These are usable optimization signals without human approval every round. Human validation, when available, calibrates later judgments rather than authorizing each step.

## Escape a local optimum

If repeated tuning fails, stop that hypothesis. Reconsider the mechanic or its representation. Offer a simplification and a different approach before adding more effects, content or progression. Keep rejected experiments and their reasons. Do not turn the rubric into a feature checklist that every genre must satisfy.
